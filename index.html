Solder math

Every cold joint was the right temperature on paper. The forum said 350, the dial said 350, and the ground plane drank the heat anyway. Alloy melting points are spec data - whether your joint flows is thermal mass, and the dial is not the joint.

The problem it solves

"What temperature should my iron be?" is the most asked question in hobby electronics, and the answer it gets - a single number - is why pads lift and joints freeze. A 63/37 joint on a small SMD pad and a lead-free joint on a ground-plane tab are different problems wearing the same dial.

Solder math splits the question the way the bench does: the alloy's published melting range (spec data), the setpoint and wattage the joint's thermal mass calls for (labeled bench guidance), and a cold-joint check that grades your actual dial against the working window - before the joint, not after.

Specs are exact; the guidance is labeled and your tips, airflow and boards will argue with it.
